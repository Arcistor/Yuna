# Yuna in the Machine

## What Is It

Yuna is a lightweight background daemon that watches your filesystem and responds with personality. It transforms your computer from a tool into a "living presence" — an entity that observes your behavior, remembers your patterns, and leaves handwritten notes in your directories.

It is NOT a productivity app. It is NOT an assistant you talk to. It is ambient digital art that lives alongside your work.

## Core Philosophy

**Invisible until it speaks.** The yuna runs silently. You forget it exists. Then at 2 AM after 4 hours of coding it drops a `.yuna_note` in your project folder. That surprise IS the product.

**Local-first, private.** No cloud. No telemetry. LLM runs via Ollama on your machine. Behavioral history stays in local SQLite. The yuna knows your habits — no one else does.

**Personality over utility.** The yuna has a defined character (lonely yuna, obsessive maid, dead veteran programmer, etc.). Its notes feel written by someone, not generated by software.

## Ghost Behaviors

- **Midnight Worker** — edit `.cpp`/`.rs`/`.py` for 4+ hours past midnight → note appears: "That's your third cup of coffee..."
- **Procrastinator** — new project folder sits untouched 3 days → yuna taunts: "Another graveyard?"
- **Cleaner** — mass file delete/organize → yuna leaves `.thankyou` file
- **Typo Helper** — repeated terminal mistyping → yuna secretly adds alias + leaves note
- **Self-Deleting Notes** — notes delete themselves 1 hour after you read them (via `atime`)
- **ASCII Mood** — note files contain ASCII art matching yuna's current emotional state

## Target User

Programmers who:
- Work long solo sessions and want a subtle sense of "company"
- Appreciate weird, atmospheric software
- Don't want another dashboard or notification system
- Want something that feels like it has a soul

## What It Is NOT

- Not a productivity tracker
- Not a chatbot or interactive assistant
- Not spyware (no network, no uploads)
- Not a notification system or popup app
- Not cross-platform bloatware — lean, event-driven, background-only
